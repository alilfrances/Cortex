- **Overriding methods are followed (B.2).** A call through a `Base*` lands on the base method, and the fix is often in a derived class that no edge leads to. No `overrides` relation is stored (the resolver never adds edges, and a relation would change `cortex_relations`, impact and dead-code output). `overridden_by` is derived at query time from the resolved `inherits` edges and the class membership the resolver uses (`callresolve.ClassIndex`, factored out of `_Index`; `overrides.find_overriders` builds it only for the classes of a hierarchy): a method of a derived class, at most 6 levels down, with the name of a base method (not constructors or destructors). When the callee walk reaches a base method it also offers up to 4 of its overriders (most task terms in the span first, then path; test/mock trees demoted) at the callee's own score and hop. `why: {type: "override", from, via, to, hop}`, compact form `override: <file>:<base method> -> <file>:<overrider>`. - **Callees and seeds are chosen by relevance (B.3).** Of a function's other-file callees the 4 most relevant are followed (a task term in the name or, beyond 3 lines, the body; else a task term in the file; else the rest; call-site order breaks ties) instead of the first 4 in call-site order. Not followed unless the name carries a task term: callees of at most 3 lines, accessor-shaped names (`get*`/`set*`/`is*`/`has*`, `*Changed`), and utilities called from more places than 99% of the called functions and at least 20 times; a one-line virtual that has overriders is still followed.
